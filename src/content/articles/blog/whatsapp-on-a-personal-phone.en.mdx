Almost every business we talk to started the same way. One person answered
WhatsApp because they were good at it, and it worked. Then it kept working, and
nobody changed it, and now the company's customer relationships live on a phone
that goes home every evening.

It is worth naming what that actually costs, because the cost is invisible right
up until the moment it isn't.

## You cannot see your own conversations

A manager cannot answer "what did we promise this customer?" without asking the
person holding the phone. There is no search, no history, no way to check
whether a quote was sent or a complaint was resolved. Decisions get made on
recollection.

## The number belongs to whoever holds it

When that employee resigns, three things leave with them: the chat history, the
contacts, and — often — the customers' habit of writing to that specific person.
Some businesses discover mid-handover that the phone number was registered
personally and cannot simply be reassigned.

## Nobody is accountable, so nobody is responsible

With a single shared phone or a stack of personal ones, there is no concept of
"assigned to". Messages get answered twice, or not at all, and both failures
look identical from the outside: silence, then confusion.

## The customer waits

This is the one that actually costs money. A customer asking "do you have this
in stock?" is buying *now*. If the answer arrives four hours later, they have
already bought it somewhere else. Not because your prices were wrong — because
somebody was driving.

## What changing it looks like

Moving to the WhatsApp Business Platform is not primarily a technology decision.
It is deciding that the number is a company asset, like the email domain:

- **One number, many agents.** Everyone answers from the same inbox, on the
  same number the customer already has.
- **Ownership per conversation.** Assigned, with a status, so "who is handling
  this" has an answer.
- **History that stays.** When someone leaves, the conversations do not.
- **Automation where it helps.** After-hours replies, and — if you want it — an
  AI agent that answers routine questions instantly and escalates the rest.

The migration itself is a couple of hours of admin, mostly waiting on Meta
verification. See [WABA vs the Business app](/information-center/waba-vs-whatsapp-business-app)
for what actually happens to the number.

## The honest counter-argument

If one person genuinely handles every message, and the volume is comfortable,
the free WhatsApp Business app is fine and you should keep using it. The
threshold is not company size — it is the moment two people need the same
number, or the moment you notice you cannot answer a question about your own
customer without asking a colleague to check their phone.
