title: Opt-in on WhatsApp — what actually counts

---

Meta's rule is short: before you send a business-initiated message on WhatsApp,
the person must have agreed to receive messages from you on WhatsApp. Everything
else is detail — but the detail is where businesses get into trouble.

## What a valid opt-in contains

Three things, together:

1. **The person's clear action.** A ticked box, a typed number, a tapped
   button. Not a pre-ticked checkbox, not silence.
2. **Your business name.** They agreed to hear from *you* specifically.
3. **That the messages come on WhatsApp.** Consent for email or SMS is not
   consent for WhatsApp.

## Where you can collect it

Meta deliberately allows opt-in to be collected almost anywhere, as long as the
three elements above are present:

- A checkbox on your website checkout or contact form.
- A field in your app or customer portal.
- In person — a form at the counter, or a signup sheet.
- Over the phone, if you record that consent was given.
- On WhatsApp itself: if a customer messages you first, that is the strongest
  signal there is.

## What does not count

- A purchased list of phone numbers.
- Numbers scraped from anywhere.
- "You accepted our terms and conditions, which mention marketing."
- Consent for SMS or email, reused for WhatsApp.
- A pre-ticked box.

## Keep the record

You should be able to answer, for any number you message: **when** consent was
given, **where**, and **what the person saw** when they gave it. If a customer
complains — or Meta asks — that record is your defence. Screenshots of the
consent surface and a timestamp per contact are enough for most businesses.

## Opting out

Honour a stop request immediately, in whatever words it arrives. "Stop",
"unsubscribe", "לא מעוניין", or simply "don't message me again" all mean the
same thing. In WeizChat, a customer marked as blocked for delivery is never
included when a campaign audience is built — the exclusion happens in the query
that assembles the audience, not as a filter someone might forget to apply.

## Why it is worth taking seriously

Beyond the policy: blocks and "report" taps feed your quality rating, and a low
rating cuts how many people you may message per day. Opt-in discipline is not
only compliance — it is what keeps the channel working.
