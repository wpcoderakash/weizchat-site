You cannot message a customer out of the blue on the WhatsApp Business Platform.
If there is no open 24-hour service window, the only way to reach someone is a
**message template** that Meta approved beforehand.

## What a template is

A template is a message with fixed text and numbered gaps for variables:

> Hi `{{1}}`, your order `{{2}}` has shipped. Track it here: `{{3}}`

You submit that structure to Meta. Once approved, you can send it thousands of
times with different values in the gaps. The wording is locked; only the
variables change.

## The three categories

Every template is submitted under a category, and the category decides both
price and how strictly it is reviewed.

| Category | For | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Utility** | Something the customer is already doing — an order, a booking, an account | Free when delivered inside an open service window |
| **Authentication** | One-time passcodes and login codes | Has its own rules and often its own rates |
| **Marketing** | Anything promotional — offers, launches, re-engagement | Always billed, and reviewed most strictly |

Submitting a marketing message as "utility" to save money is the most common
way businesses get templates rejected and quality ratings lowered. Meta
re-categorises what it disagrees with.

## Why templates get rejected

In practice, most rejections come down to a handful of causes:

- **Category mismatch.** The text is promotional but was submitted as utility.
- **Policy content.** Anything the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy prohibits.
- **Variables that could be anything.** A template that is mostly variables —
  "`{{1}}` `{{2}}` `{{3}}`" — gives reviewers no way to judge what will
  actually be sent, so they refuse it.
- **Missing context.** A message that does not make clear who is writing and
  why the customer is hearing from them.
- **Structural mistakes.** A name with capitals or spaces, variables numbered
  out of order, a placeholder that does not match its example.

The structural ones are avoidable before you submit — our
[template checker](/tools/template-checker) catches those, and tells you plainly
which rules it does not check.

## Practical advice

- Write the template as if you were the customer receiving it cold.
- Include the business name in the body, not just the header.
- Keep marketing genuinely useful and infrequent — quality rating is real, and
  a low one reduces how many people you can reach per day.
- If a template is rejected, read Meta's stated reason before rewriting. In
  WeizChat we show Meta's answer in Meta's words rather than paraphrasing it.
